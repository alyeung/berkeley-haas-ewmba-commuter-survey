# EXECUTIVE MEMO: EWMBA Commuter Parking & Policy Changes
**TO:** Dean of the Haas School of Business  
**FROM:** Evening & Weekend MBA Association (EWMBAA) Officers  
**DATE:** July 12, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Student Impact & Data Feedback on EWMBA Parking Permit Changes  

---

### Executive Summary
Following the recent announcement regarding changes to the Evening & Weekend MBA (EWMBA) parking permit policy, the EWMBAA conducted a rapid-response feedback survey. 

We received **79 detailed responses** from EWMBA students representing the Evening Blue, Evening Gold, Weekend (Axe/Oski), and Nexus/Lux (Flex) cohorts. The data shows that the elimination of parking permits is a structural barrier that directly threatens **student safety, academic participation, mental well-being, and long-term brand equity**.

---

### 1. Key Quantitative Data Highlights

*   **Vehicle Dependency:** **93.7%** of EWMBA students rely on driving (alone or in carpools) to access in-person classes. Only **2.5%** commute via public transit.
*   **Crucial to Experience:** **94.9%** of respondents agree/strongly agree that a parking pass is crucial to their school experience (Average: **4.84 / 5**).
*   **Reduced Engagement:** **92.4%** state that having to pay for parking will prevent them from attending more on-campus events and maximizing their MBA (Average: **4.72 / 5**).
*   **Unnecessary Stress:** **92.4%** agree/strongly agree that the new parking charge, combined with rising tuition, causes unnecessary extra stress and negatively impacts their student experience (Average: **4.77 / 5**).
*   **Reputational Threat:** **74.7%** of students would advise prospective applicants to factor this change negatively when choosing Haas (with **50.6%** recommending they weigh it against competing programs like Wharton SF/Stanford, and **24.1%** actively discouraging enrollment).
*   **Mandatory Fee Disconnect:** **74.7%** of students would immediately opt out of the mandatory BayPass transit fee if given the option, as it provides no practical value to their commute.

### 3. Feedback Themes & Aligned Data

#### Theme A: Breach of Trust & "Bait-and-Switch" Sentiment
Students explicitly applied and committed to Haas with the promise that parking permits were included in tuition. Revoking this benefit mid-program is viewed as a deceptive transactional change.
*   **Aligned Data:** **92.4%** of students report that this change adds unnecessary stress to their student experience. **74.7%** would actively advise prospective applicants to seriously weigh this issue against other programs.
*   **Student Voice:** *"When enrolling in the program, the parking was an expected benefit. Taking it away mid-program feels deceptive and unfair... like a bait-and-switch—joining under one set of expectations only to have those expectations altered once it is no longer practical to reconsider."*

#### Theme B: The Flawed Logic of "Program Alignment"
The administration's justification that this change "aligns" the EWMBA program with other MBA programs is seen as structurally flawed. Full-Time MBA (FTMBA) students live near campus and spend their days there; EWMBA students commute hours after a full workday.
*   **Aligned Data:** **50.6%** of students commute directly after their workday, and **58.2%** cite BART/transit limitations as the reason they are forced to drive.
*   **Student Voice:** *"FTMBA students live closer to campus and spend the majority of their day in classes... Evening students must commute after their workday, and live scattered across the Bay Area. Given this, it clearly does not make sense to align these two programs on an issue such as parking."*

#### Theme C: Disproportionate Fee Burden (Mandatory Fees vs. Zero-Sum)
EWMBA students are charged mandatory student fees for campus amenities (gyms, recreational facilities, transit passes) they cannot use due to their evening/Saturday schedule. Charging extra for parking on top of this feels like "nickel-and-diming."
*   **Aligned Data:** **74.7%** of students would choose to opt out of the mandatory BayPass fee if given the option, showing the massive disconnect between mandatory fees and actual student utility.
*   **Student Voice:** *"Requiring EWMBA students to pay mandatory fees for amenities that most of them seldom use [like gym facilities], and then charging extra for parking on top of those fees, is difficult to justify... it makes EWMBA students feel like second-class members of the Haas community and a source of additional revenue."*

#### Theme D: Safety & Logistical Disruption
Public transit at 10:00 PM is unsafe and impractical. Sparse train schedules force students to sprint out of class, eliminating networking/socializing, which is a core value of the MBA experience.
*   **Aligned Data:** **60.8%** of students report that parking or transit delays have directly disrupted their academic schedule (being late, missing lectures, or missing group meetings).
*   **Student Voice:** *"Taking BART back to SF after class at 10pm can be unsafe and tiring... Southbound BART leaves every 30 minutes, which means I cannot network with classmates, professors, or guests at all for fear of missing the train and getting stranded."*

---

### 4. Proposed Compromise Solutions
We propose evaluating the following win-win compromises to balance the university's goals with the student experience:

1.  **Class-Time Parking Permits (Highly Recommended):**  
    Provide EWMBA students with restricted parking permits valid only during class hours: **Weekdays after 5:00 PM** and **Saturdays 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM**. This protects student class time while preventing any impact on daytime campus parking capacity.
2.  **Mandatory Fee Reallocation/Opt-Out:**  
    Allow EWMBA students to "opt out" of unused campus facility fees (such as RSF gym fees) and reallocate those funds directly to offset their parking costs.
3.  **Late-Night BART/Embarcadero Shuttle:**  
    Establish a dedicated Haas shuttle to San Francisco/Embarcadero immediately after class ends at 10:00 PM. This provides a safe, direct transit option during off-peak hours and increases the utility of the mandated BayPass.
4.  **Reciprocal Cohort Benefits:**  
    If EWMBA is strictly aligned with other cohorts on parking, equalize corresponding benefits—such as providing weekend dinner coverage (similar to weekday cohort lunch coverage) or offering tuition discounts on summer courses.
